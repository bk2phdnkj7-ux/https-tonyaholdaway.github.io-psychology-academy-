Week 6 Psychology Mission

The Conditioning Vault

Pavlov's signals. Skinner's consequences. Your escape.

The Academy's learning records are locked inside a vault. Seven research rooms hold the missing clues. Investigate how behavior changes, recover each evidence card, and use what you learn to reopen the archive.

Academy Atmosphere

Optional instrumental music. All clues are also written; sound is never required.

Music is off.

Mission Briefing

Open any room, explore its evidence, and solve both locks to earn a clue letter. Collect seven letters, then solve the final case and arrange the letters into the seven-letter word that completes “A response acquired through experience has been _______.”

No timer and no penalty for retries. Read the feedback, use hints, and try again. Allow about 25–40 minutes. Demonstrations are simplified teaching models, not real experimental data.

Progress and notes are saved in this browser when storage is available. Download your notebook before changing devices or using a shared computer. This activity does not automatically submit a Canvas grade.

Your Learning Goals

  • Distinguish classical, operant, and observational learning.
  • Identify stimuli and responses in Pavlov's conditioning model.
  • Explain extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalization, and discrimination.
  • Classify consequences and reinforcement schedules.
  • Use shaping and modeling to explain a new skill.

Pavlov was a physiologist whose research influenced behaviorism. Skinner studied operant conditioning. Bandura's work extends this mission to observational learning.
